Stage 3 Bench Assembly

Seven steps, each ending in a measurement GATE you don't pass until it holds. If a GATE doesn't hold, the fault is in that step — building the next step hides it, it doesn't fix it.

Read this first

615 V kills — and this board is NOT isolated

The board is connected to your computer over USB. If you connect it to a mains-referenced circuit (such as the primary side of a non-isolated SMPS) you carry mains voltage into your computer. Only the isolated secondary side, or fully isolated operation (battery + WiFi, USB unplugged).

But floating it is not enough. The fault analysis (B15/D2) measured it: floating on battery saves the computer, it does NOT SAVE YOU — the board rises to mains potential at that moment and every point on it is dangerous. 615 V is 14.6 times the limit for a floating instrument. So for mains-referenced measurements:

  • An insulated enclosure is MANDATORY — no point of the board may be reachable by hand, probe tips included
  • For reinforced insulation on perfboard, skip 6 holes (13.70 mm copper to copper — pad diameter subtracted; IEC 60664 creepage 12.6 mm). Layout plan: Layout
  • Don't touch the board while it is energized; when the measurement is done, first disconnect the circuit, then remove the probe

Keep the high-voltage lead physically separate from the measurement lead, use a different color, work one-handed.

615 V on perfboard — the hole pitch is not enough

The hole pitch is 2.54 mm. Place the 6-resistor HV chain skipping holes, with gaps between them; preferably build it in the air directly on the banana jack. This is a separate point of care, independent of soldering skill.

To buy

Resistors must be METAL FILM — carbon film makes the design pointless

In a 601:1 divider the resistor type matters more than its value. Total error with a 20 °C room swing and 102 V per resistor: carbon film 4.10%, metal film 1%/50 ppm 0.41%. For comparison: ADS after calibration 0.03%, reference multimeter 0.5%.

Two terms cannot be calibrated out: VCR depends on voltage, so it is not linear (calibrate at 12 V and measure 600 V and the error comes back), and carbon film's 1–3% drift over 1000 hours makes the calibration stale within weeks — while the number still looks convincing.

Telling them apart: metal film is usually a blue/green body, 5 bands (1%); carbon film is beige, 4 bands (5%).

Tools needed

  • Multimeter — it is the calibration reference; the board's absolute accuracy cannot exceed it
  • Adjustable power supply (a 12 V adapter is enough to start with)
  • ±12 V rail — the TL072s (U5, U8) run from it. A single supply won't do: their outputs cannot get down to 0.69 V.
  • +5 V — the LM358s (U3, U4) run from it

Decoupling capacitors — not optional

A local 100nF on every op-amp supply pin: C9 (U3, +5V), C10 (U4, +5V), C11/C12 (U5, ±12V), C13/C14 (U8, ±12V), C15 (ADS rail). All seven are in stock.

The op-amp outputs drive a capacitive load (the Sallen-Key capacitors); with a high supply impedance this turns into oscillation. The TL072's slew rate is 13 V/µs — sudden current demand must be met by the local capacitor, not by 20 cm of supply wire.

01

Vref rail — everything rests on it

Build the TL431 rail and the Vref derived from it. All bidirectional measurement rests on Vref: the bottom ends of the dividers go here, not to GND.

  +3V3 ──[ 220R ]──┬── TL431 cath. ──┬── TL_RAY 2.495 V
                   │       (U1)      │
                   │                 └──[100nF]── GND
                   └── TL431 REF (tied to cathode)

  TL_RAY ──[ 10K ]──┬──[ 22K ]── GND
                    │
                 U3A follower ── VREF 1.7153 V

Vref CANNOT BE UNBUFFERED. The current of the two dividers (~290 µA) flowing out of the 6.9 kΩ Thevenin resistance of an unbuffered divider would shift it by up to 2 V.

U3 = LM358, powered from +5 V. At 3.3 V its output cannot swing high enough; at +12 V it would stress the ADS.

GATE — don't proceed until it passes

Measure 2.495 V ±2% at the TL431 cathode and 1.7153 V ±10 mV at the U3A output. Vref must stay the same when measured with the multimeter under load (dividers connected) — if it doesn't shift, the buffer is working.

02

ESP32-S3 + I²C — both ADS must show up

Power the ADS1115 modules from 3.3 V (at 5 V, VIH would be 3.5 V and the ESP32's 3.3 V outputs would not reach it). Addresses:

ModuleADDRAddressRole
ADS #1 (U6)GND0x48CURRENT, AIN0-AIN1 differential
ADS #2 (U7)+3V30x49VOLTAGE, AIN0-1 and AIN2-3

Pull-ups: R24/R25 = 2.7K — NOT 4.7K. With two modules, 4.7K gives a rise time of 308 ns, and the Fast-mode limit is 300 ns. 2.7K passes with both two and three modules (223 / 190 ns).

The modules have their own 10K pull-ups; the 2.7K ends up in parallel with them.

GATE — don't proceed until it passes

Flash the firmware, open the serial port at 115200 and type #. Both 0x48 and 0x49 must show up. If not, the problem is in the wiring: SDA/SCL swapped, ADDR floating, or no pull-ups. Don't move on to calibration.

03

Current channel — shunt, Kelvin, bidirectional

Fit the shunt in a socket. Take the Kelvin (4-wire) sense leads from the shunt body itself, not from the trace carrying the current.

The Kelvin leads go to TWO places: the ADS branch (R18/R19 100R, then the B15/F2 protection resistors R38/R39 1K) and the fast path's difference amplifier (R27/R29). The fast path is tapped before R38/R39.

B16 — the filter sits in the ADS's OWN branch: C4 is now 1nF (RF only). The anti-aliasing filter C18 1µF + C19 220nF + C20 100nF = 1.32 µF goes after R38/R39, across the ADS input pins — short legs, close to the pins. Had the filter been placed at the C4 node, it would also have closed the fast path's bandwidth (exactly what happened before B16: I_HIZLI stopped at 7.8 kHz while the scope channel reached 16.55 kHz).

Why this value: to match the time constant of the voltage channel. Without the match the wattmeter breaks down on reactive loads — before B16 the V/I phase difference at 50 Hz was −41.6°, a 155% error at PF=0.5. On resistive loads the difference cancels out, which is why it went unnoticed.

Read the tolerance letter on the capacitors (J = 5%, K = 10%). B16 section 4: tolerance is now the dominant error source. With 10% parts, 5% of boards can be worse than 13% at PF=0.5 — hence the firmware needs a per-range phase calibration (with a resistive load, no reference instrument required).

GATE — don't proceed until it passes

Connect the input to 0 V and type z (voltage zero — it measures the actual Vref and subtracts it). Then apply a known voltage and calibrate with g<volts>. Then reverse the input — the reading must be negative with the same magnitude. There must be no clipping at +32 V and −32 V.

05

HIGH voltage channel — ±613 V

  HV_INPUT ──[ 6× 820K series ]──┬──[ 8.2K ]── VREF
                                 │
                              [R17 22K]── C3 100nF ── VREF
                                 │
                            U4A follower ── ADS #2 AIN2   (AIN3 = VREF)

Bottom leg 8.2K, one hundredth of the chain. 6 × R / (R/100) gives a division ratio of exactly 601 for any R; the largest pair where both R and R/100 exist in the series is 820K / 8.2K, which also gives the highest input impedance.

Why 6 of them: the maximum working voltage of a 1/4 W metal film resistor is 200 V. With 4 of them, each resistor would drop 154 V and full scale would stop at 410 V.

Why 820K and not 2.2M: as long as the bottom leg is one hundredth of the chain, the ratio stays exactly 601.0, so 820K / 2.2M / 6.8M would all give the same range and the same step. The only difference is surface leakage: on perfboard, a leakage path forming in parallel with the chain pulls the division ratio down, and the error is directly proportional to the chain resistance. With 10 GΩ of leakage, 820K gives a 0.049% error, 2.2M 0.132%, 6.8M 0.408%. Leakage cannot be calibrated out — it changes from day to day with humidity, the same argument that rules out carbon film. What 2.2M buys is input impedance (13.2 MΩ versus 4.9 MΩ), but what this channel measures (rectified mains, DC bus, SMPS output) are sources in the milliohm range; there the difference is not measurable.

Keep the board clean: solder paste residue and fingerprints around the chain are leakage paths. After soldering, wipe with isopropyl alcohol and let it dry thoroughly — on this divider cleanliness is an accuracy parameter.

DANGER

LOW VOLTAGE FIRST. Apply 12 V and check the reading — with a 18.8 mV step, 12 V ≈ 639 codes, it won't get lost in the noise. Then 100 V, then step up gradually. At every step hands away, work one-handed.

06

Oscilloscope channel + Sallen-Key

Separate input, separate divider (100K/2.7K, ratio 38.037) — not the same node as the voltage channels.

B19 — the channel is BIDIRECTIONAL. The bottom end of R23 goes to VREF, not GND (the solution the voltage channels already use). Zero input = VREF, negative input goes down, positive input goes up. Range -63.5 … +46.8 V, step 28.8 mV. Before, it was unidirectional 0…45.5 V with a 11.9 mV step — the price is resolution.

⚠️ Don't solder R23 to GND. In the schematic it goes to VREF; fitted to GND, the channel silently stays unidirectional and readings shift by 64 V.

Sallen-Key anti-aliasing filter (U5A): R21/R22 = 6.8K, C5 = 2nF (2× 1nF in parallel), C6 = 1nF → f0 = 16.55 kHz, Q = 0.7071.

🔴 B20 correction: this line used to say R22/R23 and C7; both were wrong and an assembly trap. The truth, verified against the netlist: R23 is the bottom leg of the divider (2.7K, to VREF — the subject of the warning above), the Sallen-Key resistor is R21; C7 belongs to U5B (the fast current Sallen-Key), U5A's is C5. Fitting 6.8K at R23 would both leave the scope channel unidirectional and break its scaling.

The single-pole RC in the old design sat at 25 kHz — with a per-channel Nyquist of 20.83 kHz that is above Nyquist, so it filtered nothing. A 100 kHz SMPS ripple would fold down to 16.7 kHz completely unattenuated and look real.

Protection: 10K series + 2× BAT85 (D1 on top to +3V3, D2 below to GND). NO 1N4148 — SPICE measured it: 25% of the fault current goes into the ADS's internal ESD diode. With BAT85 the pin stays at −0.268 V.

B18/F12 — the series resistor is 10K, not 2.7K. This resistor sets the current fed back into the dead +3V3 rail while the board is unpowered (USB unplugged but 24 V connected). With 2.7K the rail rose to 3.582 V — the ESP32 supply pin limit is 3.60 V, a margin of 18 mV. With 10K + R41 (1K bleeder resistor) the rail stays at 1.67 V. No loss in any range; the cost on the ADC side is 0.5 mV of leakage (<1 LSB) and 330 ns of settling (1/73 of the sample interval).

Don't forget R41: 1K between +3V3 and GND. It looks minor, but it is the board's only protection while unpowered — and it is not tied to the TL431.

GATE — don't proceed until it passes

Apply a known square wave (an NE555 or the ESP32's own PWM). Type ta (auto setup) — frequency and duty must come out right. On a flat line a measurement must not be possible (frequency 0).

07

Fast current path — real power and PF

  SONT_P ──[10K]──┬── U8A(+)      difference amp, G = 4.7
                  └──[47K]── VREF   (REF at VREF → bidirectional)
  SONT_N ──[10K]──┬── U8A(−)
                  └──[47K]── out
  out ── U5B Sallen-Key 16.55 kHz ── 10.0K ── 2× BAT85 ── GPIO5

Gain 4.7 (47K/10K): the full-scale shunt voltage is 294.6 mV, i.e. wider than the ADS's 256 mV — the ADS clips first, not the fast path.

U5B sat unused until Stage 3; it is now the fast current Sallen-Key.

GATE — don't proceed until it passes

Connect a resistive load and type w. PF ≈ 1.00 must come out and the interface must say "no alignment difference (resistive)". Then add a capacitor/inductor — PF must drop and the interface must show by how many percent the alignment corrected it.

⚠️ Look at the window line in the output: below 1 cycle at 50 Hz the bias is large. This number is not for absolute watts, it is for power factor and waveform.
