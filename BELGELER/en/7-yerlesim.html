Which part goes in which hole of the perfboard, which wire runs from where to where — step by step, in LEGO-instructions order.

Assembly works like LEGO instructions: 9 main steps (0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8), each split into small sub-steps. Every sub-step has a few parts or a few solder traces; every main step ends with a GATE measurement, and you don't move on until it passes. Order: low-profile parts first, then tall ones; any part that can go in backwards (electrolytic, diode, socket, transistor, regulator, header) always gets its own sub-step; solder traces come after the parts, wires after the traces.

The boards are pad-per-hole perfboard. The main board (A) is cut from a 13×23 cm board (45×90 holes) down to 45×45 holes; the HV chain (B) sits on a separate 5×5 cm board (18×18). The shunt, J3, J7 and Q1 are not on the perfboard: perfboard copper cannot carry load currents of up to 11.5 A. They live in the enclosure (panel jacks, shunt mount, heatsink); only the Kelvin leads, the single star-ground wire and the gate wire come to the board. The 615 V input never reaches board A: from the chain on board B only the ~1.7 V bottom node comes over to A.

Coordinates: column = letter, row = number; A1 is top left seen from the component side. The solder-side drawing is mirrored — when you flip the board, the same letter lands over the same hole. Insulated wires run on the solder side; their ends are soldered to the lead or pad there.

Resistors — type and power

Power: all 1/4 W, only R40 is 1/2 W. Design calculation (tasarim3 §12): the most heavily loaded resistor is R20, at 15% of 1/4 W; the highest voltage is 102 V across an 820K in the chain (51% of the 200 V limit of a 1/4 W part). Don't use a larger body: its parasitic capacitance cuts the scope bandwidth.

Type: the 15 resistors that set the measurement ratio must be metal film 1% — carbon film's voltage-dependent value and its 1–3% drift over 1000 hours cannot be calibrated out (§11). For 8 resistors metal film is recommended (zero-point and filter stability); the rest can be standard carbon. The direnc.net packs in stock (blue body, 5 bands) are metal film; the motorobit packs and loose resistors have a beige body, 4 bands = carbon.

Capacitors — type

There are two kinds of small ceramic in stock, and they are not interchangeable:

  • Ceramic disc — flat, round, dark orange/brown disc; the leads come straight down (in stock: C049 1nF, 10 pcs). Wide tolerance, value drifts with temperature. Only where the value is not critical: TL431 stability (C1), Kelvin RC (C4), Sallen-Key (C5–C8).
  • Multilayer ceramic (MLCC) — blob-shaped, light yellow/mustard body; the leads come out from under the body, close together (in stock: C051 100nF 2.5 mm, C008 100nF 5 mm, C052 220nF). For decoupling capacitors and filters: the tolerance of C2/C3 and of C19/C20 enters the V/I matching (B16).
  • Film (polyester) — rectangular box or dipped drop; for C18 the 15 mm pitch C022.
  • Electrolytic — cylinder, polarised: long lead +, the stripe on the body − (C16/C17, C035).

Telling them apart: a disc's two leads leave from the edge of the body and the body is a thin flat tablet; a multilayer's body is a thick blob and the leads come out from underneath. Colours vary from batch to batch — go by the shape. The 1nF discs may have 5 mm lead spacing; the plan wants 2.5 mm, so bend the leads closer.

What this plan guarantees, and what it doesn't

The check (uretim/yerlesim3.py) rebuilds the copper connectivity from the geometry and compares it one-to-one with the schematic netlist: no opens, no shorts, at every main assembly step as well. Bodies don't overlap, no lead lands under another body; the Kelvin leads are alone on the board, ground joins the power path at a single point; every copper pair above 50 V meets the IEC 60664-1 Table F.4 spacing; decoupling capacitors are close to their pins.

The sub-step order is checked too: every part, trace, wire and cable is in exactly one sub-step; no trace or wire fills with solder a hole a later part still needs; within a step, low parts come before tall ones; parts that can go in backwards get their own sub-step; every main step ends with a GATE check.

What it doesn't guarantee: part dimensions are estimates — the fuse clip's lead spacing, the 68 µF's diameter (8 mm assumed), film C18's lead pitch (15 mm). Part heights are approximate too (used only for ordering). On the bench, seat each part on the board and check the holes; if something doesn't fit, say so and the plan will be regenerated. The quality of the copper (cold joints, bridges) can only be tested with a multimeter.
